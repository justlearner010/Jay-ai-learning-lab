### Propositions

**Definition** : Propositions is a self-contained statement that only has true or false

**Sign**
$∧$ : and
$∨$ : or
$¬$: not
$≡$ : always true

**Predicates**

A predicate is  like a proposition but with variables inside.

**Quantifiers**

- **Existential Quantifier**:  $(∃x∈S)(P(x))$ :means "P(x) is True for some x in S"

- **Universal Quantifier**: $(∀x∈S)(P(x))$: means"P(x) is True for all x in S"



De Morgan’s law for quantifiers (same idea: move negation in and flip op):

$¬(∃x ∈ S)(P(x)) ⇐⇒ ∀(x ∈ S)¬P(x)$

The key point of **De Morgan's Law** is:

> **When you move a negation inside a logical expression, you must flip the operator.**
